Motivation

The KV cache is the memory/runtime bottleneck of long-context LLMs. Query-dependent compression (SnapKV, H2O, PyramidKV) cannot reuse one cache across different queries over the same context. Context-reconstruction attention (KVzip) is query-agnostic — one compressed cache is reusable — but it adds huge prefill-time overhead: for a 124K-token context it adds 34 s+ on top of a ~14 s prefill. Can query-agnostic importance be computed without scoring all attention heads?

Key Observation   &   Method

HybridKV removes structurally redundant head-level computation while keeping context-adaptive token decisions:
  • Offline: aggregate reconstruction scores on a tiny calibration set (10 contexts) and pre-select a fraction k of salient heads (head-retention ratio).
  • Deployment: drop KV of pruned heads, then apply token-level reconstruction pruning only within the retained heads.
  • Stays query-agnostic — one compressed cache reused across many queries.

Results

Efficiency & Takeaway

Head-dominant structure makes accurate query-agnostic KV compression cheap: pre-select heads once, prune tokens within them at deployment.
  • Matches full context-reconstruction accuracy.
  • Up to 36% lower compression overhead.
  • Consistent across 3 long-context LLMs & 4 benchmarks.
